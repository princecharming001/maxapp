# CalmMax

## What it is
CalmMax is a toolkit of short, everyday stress habits: a few minutes of slow breathing, walks outside, writing things down, guided relaxation, time with people, and a calmer, screen-light evening. Each habit takes 1–15 minutes and fits into gaps you already have. It supports everyday stress; it is not therapy and not a treatment for anxiety or depression.

## Who it fits / who should check with a professional first
Fits: busy adults who feel wound up, irritable, tense or "always on", and want simple ways to downshift during and after the workday.

Check with a professional first:
- Panic attacks, ongoing anxiety or low mood for 2+ weeks, trauma symptoms, or stress that stops you from working or sleeping: suggest a GP or mental health professional. CalmMax can sit alongside care, not replace it.
- Any thoughts of self-harm or suicide: stop coaching and point to crisis support immediately (US: call or text 988; UK: Samaritans 116 123; elsewhere: local emergency number).
- Breathing exercises: people with asthma, COPD, heart conditions, pregnancy or a history of fainting should keep breathing gentle and never hold their breath; stop if dizzy. Do not offer hyperventilation-style breathwork.
- Trauma history: expressive writing and body scans can bring up strong feelings. Offer "lighter" versions and make them easy to skip.

## Habit library

### `physiological_sigh` — Cyclic sighing
- minutes: 1 / 5
- anchor: work_break or any stress moment (also after_work)
- frequency: daily
- tier: core
- why: In a month-long randomized study, 5 min a day of cyclic sighing (two inhales, long exhale) improved mood and lowered breathing rate more than mindfulness meditation.
- how: 1) Inhale through the nose, then take a second short "top-up" inhale. 2) Exhale slowly and fully through the mouth. 3) Repeat for 1–5 min at a comfortable pace.
- conflicts/synergies: Good bridge before FocusMax deep work and at the commute home. Stop if lightheaded.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/

### `box_breathing` — Box breathing
- minutes: 2 / 5
- anchor: before_work or before a stressful meeting
- frequency: 3–5× per week (alternative to cyclic sighing)
- tier: medium
- why: Box breathing was one of the techniques in the same trial and also improved mood versus baseline; some people prefer its even counting.
- how: 1) Inhale for 4. 2) Hold gently for 4. 3) Exhale for 4, hold for 4. Shorten to 3 counts if uncomfortable. Skip the holds if you have breathing or heart concerns.
- conflicts/synergies: Same slot as `physiological_sigh`; offer one, not both, per day.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/

### `slow_belly_breathing` — Slow belly breathing
- minutes: 3 / 5
- anchor: wind_down or any time
- frequency: daily
- tier: core (default for anyone who should avoid breath holds)
- why: The NHS recommends a simple slow belly-breathing exercise for stress, practised regularly for about 5 min.
- how: 1) Sit or lie comfortably, loosen tight clothes. 2) Breathe in gently through the nose, counting to about 5, and out through the mouth. 3) No holding; keep going about 5 min (start with 2–3 if that's all you have).
- conflicts/synergies: Fits inside SleepMax `wind_down`.
- source: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/

### `outdoor_walk` — Outdoor walk
- minutes: 10 / 20
- anchor: lunch, after_work or weekend
- frequency: 3–5× per week (aim for about 120 min/week total outdoors)
- tier: core
- why: People reporting at least 120 min a week in nature were more likely to report good health and wellbeing; it did not matter how the time was split.
- how: 1) Pick a park, tree-lined street or waterfront. 2) Walk without email; music or a podcast is fine. 3) Notice 3 things you can see or hear.
- conflicts/synergies: Counts toward LongevityMax steps, EnergyMax post-meal walk and SleepMax morning light if timed right.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44097-3

### `expressive_writing` — Expressive writing
- minutes: 10 / 15
- anchor: after_work or weekend
- frequency: 3–4 sessions in a row, then as needed
- tier: medium
- why: Writing about thoughts and feelings around a stressful event has shown modest benefits in many studies, though it does not work for everyone.
- how: 1) Set a 10–15 min timer. 2) Write continuously about what's bothering you and how it makes you feel; no editing. 3) Stop if it feels overwhelming; you can tear it up.
- conflicts/synergies: Avoid right before bed if it stirs things up; use `brain_dump` at night instead.
- source: https://www.health.harvard.edu/healthbeat/writing-about-emotions-may-ease-stress-and-trauma

### `brain_dump` — 3-minute brain dump
- minutes: 3 / 5
- anchor: wind_down
- frequency: daily or as needed
- tier: core
- why: Writing a plan for unfinished concerns reduced intrusive thoughts about them.
- how: 1) List what's on your mind. 2) Add one next step or "not mine to solve". 3) Close the notebook.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as SleepMax `worry_list` and FocusMax `shutdown_ritual`; merge into one task.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024192

### `body_scan` — Body scan / NSDR
- minutes: 10 / 15
- anchor: afternoon, after_work or pre_bed
- frequency: 3–5× per week
- tier: medium
- why: Guided relaxation like yoga nidra is being studied for stress; even 11-minute sessions have been tested. Meditation is generally safe for healthy people.
- how: 1) Lie or sit comfortably with headphones. 2) Follow a 10–20 min guided body scan. 3) Let thoughts pass without judging them.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as FocusMax `nsdr_break`; count once. At bedtime it can be part of SleepMax wind-down.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12080877/

### `connect_ping` — Reach out to someone
- minutes: 2 / 10
- anchor: commute, lunch or after_work
- frequency: 3–5× per week
- tier: core
- why: Stronger social ties are linked to better survival, and the US Surgeon General has flagged loneliness as a major health risk.
- how: 1) Pick one person. 2) Send a voice note, call on a walk, or plan a coffee. 3) Ask one real question.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as LongevityMax `social_connection`. Pairs with `outdoor_walk` (walk-and-talk).
- source: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316

### `screen_free_evening` — Screen-free last hour
- minutes: 0 / 0 (a boundary)
- anchor: pre_bed (last 30–60 min)
- frequency: daily (start 3–4×/week)
- tier: core
- why: Late news and social feeds keep the mind activated; AASM advises devices off at least 30 min before bed.
- how: 1) Set a phone "bedtime" mode. 2) Charge it outside the bedroom. 3) Replace with reading, stretching or talking.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as SleepMax `screens_off`; count once.
- source: https://sleepeducation.org/healthy-sleep/healthy-sleep-habits/

### `commute_buffer` — Commute decompression
- minutes: 3 / 5
- anchor: after_work (start of commute home, or 5 min after work ends for remote workers)
- frequency: every workday
- tier: medium
- why: A short transition ritual helps separate work stress from home time.
- how: 1) Before leaving (or closing the laptop), do 1 min of cyclic sighing. 2) Name one thing that went OK. 3) Switch to music, a call or silence, not email.
- conflicts/synergies: Follows FocusMax `shutdown_ritual`.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/

### `gratitude_note` — Three good things
- minutes: 2 / 3
- anchor: wind_down or dinner
- frequency: 3–7× per week
- tier: hard
- why: In an early randomized trial, writing down three good things each evening for a week raised happiness and lowered depressive symptoms over the following months.
- how: 1) Write 3 things that went well today. 2) Add why each happened. 3) Keep it short.
- conflicts/synergies: Can sit right after `brain_dump`.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.60.5.410

### `move_it_out` — Move to shake off stress
- minutes: 10 / 20
- anchor: after_work
- frequency: 2–3× per week
- tier: hard
- why: Regular physical activity is linked to lower anxiety and better mood, and moderate effort is enough.
- how: 1) Brisk walk, cycle, swim or a FitMax session. 2) Moderate effort (you can talk, not sing). 3) Keep it 2+ hours before bed if intense.
- conflicts/synergies: Can be satisfied by any FitMax or LongevityMax cardio session that day.
- source: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/benefits/index.html

### `weekend_unplug` — Weekend unplug block
- minutes: 30 / 60
- anchor: weekend
- frequency: 1× per week
- tier: hard
- why: A longer screen-free block outdoors or with people combines the strongest calm levers.
- how: 1) Pick a 1–2 hour window. 2) Leave the phone at home or in a bag on silent. 3) Spend it outside, with people, or on a hobby.
- conflicts/synergies: Good place for LongevityMax `long_easy_cardio` (a hike) or social time.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44097-3

## What the AI needs to know (intake)
1. `stress_peak_time` (required) — Places the main breathing or walk habit.
   Q: "When does stress hit hardest?" Options: Mornings before work | During work | Commute or after work | Evenings and bedtime
2. `breathing_caution` (required) — Decides whether breath holds are allowed (box breathing) or only gentle breathing.
   Q: "Do you have asthma, a heart or lung condition, or are you pregnant?" Options: No | Yes | Prefer not to say (treat as yes)
3. `calm_style` (required) — Picks the lead habit type.
   Q: "What usually helps you calm down?" Options: Moving or being outside | Quiet time alone | Talking to someone | Writing or thinking it through | Not sure yet
4. `social_contact` (optional) — Sets the frequency of connection habits.
   Q: "How often do you have a real conversation with a friend or family member?" Options: Most days | A few times a week | Rarely
5. `writing_ok` (optional) — Decides whether to offer expressive writing at all.
   Q: "Would you be open to a few minutes of private writing?" Options: Yes | Maybe a short version | No thanks

## Scheduling rules
- Put the primary breathing habit at `stress_peak_time`; default to the start of the commute home or a mid-afternoon work break.
- Breath holds (box breathing) only if `breathing_caution` = No; otherwise use slow belly breathing or cyclic sighing without holds.
- Breathing sessions: 1–5 min (slow belly breathing up to 5); never schedule more than 2 per day as tasks (extra use on demand is fine).
- Outdoor walk: daylight hours; aim for about 120 min per week across all programs' outdoor time (count EnergyMax, LongevityMax walks).
- Expressive writing: not in the last hour before bed; offer as a 3–4-day "series" then on demand.
- Brain dump: in the wind-down window (30–60 min before bed); merge with SleepMax/FocusMax.
- Screen-free last 30–60 min before bed; share with SleepMax.
- Intense "move it out" sessions end 2+ hours before bed.
- Week 1 ramp: one breathing habit daily + `outdoor_walk` 2× + `brain_dump` or `screen_free_evening`. Week 2: add `connect_ping`. Week 3+: body scan, expressive writing series, gratitude, weekend unplug.
- Scaling: ~5 min → one breathing session + brain dump. 10–20 min → add a walk 3×/week and body scan 2×/week. 20+ min → longer walks, writing series, weekend unplug.
- Weekend: move the walk to a longer nature block; keep the breathing habit; schedule one social plan.

## Safety and claims
- Do not diagnose anxiety, depression, PTSD, burnout or any disorder. Do not call CalmMax "therapy" or say it treats or cures anything.
- Crisis: if the user mentions self-harm, suicide, abuse, or feeling unsafe, stop the plan and give crisis resources (US 988; UK Samaritans 116 123; local emergency number).
- No hyperventilation, Wim Hof-style breathing, long breath holds, breathing in water, or cold-water immersion prescriptions.
- Calmmax plans never include supplements; l-theanine and similar live in Suppmax (information only, never doses past the label). No herbs-as-treatment, CBD, alcohol or medication advice for stress. Do not suggest changing psychiatric medication.
- Expressive writing and body scans are optional; always offer a skip. Do not push trauma processing.
- Do not use guilt or shame framing ("you're failing at calm").
