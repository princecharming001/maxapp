# Suppmax

## What it is
Suppmax turns a supplement routine into a short, safe daily schedule: what to take, when to take it, and what not to mix. Its key job is to stay in sync with the user's other programs. When the user runs Sleepmax, Calmmax, Focusmax, Energymax, Fitmax (trainmax), Skin & Hair (skinhairmax) or Longevitymax, Suppmax adds that program's single default supplement (its `primary for` pick), as long as nothing in the user's safety answers rules it out. When a program is removed, its synced supplement goes too. Suppmax is food-first and evidence-honest: many supplements have small or uncertain effects, and the doc says so. It never treats, cures or prevents disease, and it is not a replacement for a doctor or pharmacist.

## Who it fits / who should check with a professional first
Fits: adults who already take, or want to try, a small number of common supplements and want the timing, spacing and duplicates sorted out; people running other Superpower programs who want the matching supplement added and removed automatically.

Check with a professional first (the app should suggest this, not diagnose):
- Pregnant, trying to conceive, or breastfeeding: most items here lack safety data in pregnancy. Only a clinician-recommended prenatal plan applies; Suppmax should not add anything else.
- Anyone on regular prescription medication, especially blood thinners, antidepressants (SSRIs and others), sedatives or sleep medicines, blood pressure or heart medicines, diabetes medicines, thyroid medicine, immunosuppressants, seizure medicines or stimulant (ADHD) medicines. A pharmacist can check the whole list in a few minutes.
- Kidney or liver disease: several items (magnesium, creatine, protein powder, electrolytes, ashwagandha) are not appropriate without medical advice.
- Surgery in the next 2 weeks: omega-3, ashwagandha and other items should usually be paused; follow the surgical team.
- Under 18: Suppmax is built for adults. Do not schedule melatonin, creatine, caffeine pairings, ashwagandha, rhodiola or iron for minors.
- Anyone who suspects a deficiency (very tired, pale, hair shedding, numbness or tingling, restrictive diet): get bloodwork first. Iron in particular is never added without a test.
- Ongoing insomnia, anxiety, low mood, fatigue or hair loss: these can have medical causes. A supplement should not delay seeing a clinician.

## Habit library

### `melatonin` — Low-dose melatonin
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: wind_down (30–60 min before bed)
- frequency: nightly for up to 4 weeks, then a week off and a review (or only on travel nights)
- tier: medium
- programs: sleepmax
- primary for: sleepmax
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, blood thinners, sedatives, SSRIs, blood pressure meds, diabetes meds, seizure meds, immunosuppressants, autoimmune condition, liver disease
- dose note: Studied amounts range from about 0.3 to 5 mg, and many store products are 5–10 mg, more than most people need. Most people start at the low end, 0.5–3 mg; take no more than the serving on your label. It is meant for short-term use.
- timing: 30–60 min before your usual bedtime, at the same time each night, with lights already dim. Taking it earlier (up to about 3 hours before bed) may help more if falling asleep is the problem; ask the chat to adjust. Do not drive after taking it.
- why: Melatonin is a body-clock signal more than a knockout pill. A meta-analysis found it helped people fall asleep about 7 min faster and sleep about 8 min longer; a 2024 dose-response analysis found effects level off around 4 mg. The AASM suggests against it for long-term insomnia, so it fits best for a late-running body clock, jet lag or a short reset alongside Sleepmax habits. Products often contain far more or less than the label says, so a third-party tested tablet matters.
- how: 1) Choose a 0.5–3 mg tablet with a USP or NSF seal (gummies are the least accurate). 2) Take one serving at the set time, then start your wind-down. 3) After 4 weeks, take a week off and see if you still need it.
- conflicts/synergies: Supports Sleepmax `wind_down`, `screens_off` and `fixed_wake_time`; never replaces them. Do not combine with "PM" pain relievers, antihistamine sleep aids, alcohol or other sleep products. Overrides the Sleepmax doc's "no supplements" rule only inside Suppmax.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3656905/

### `l_theanine` — L-theanine for calm
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: afternoon (or 30–60 min before a known stressful part of the day; wind_down if evenings are worst)
- frequency: daily
- tier: core
- programs: calmmax
- primary for: calmmax
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, blood pressure meds, sedatives
- dose note: Most studies used 200 mg a day (range about 200–400 mg). Labels are usually 100–200 mg per capsule; take the serving on your label.
- timing: 30–60 min before the stressful stretch of your day, with or without food. Non-drowsy, so it can be taken in the daytime.
- why: In a 4-week randomized trial, 200 mg a day lowered stress, anxiety and sleep-complaint scores in healthy adults compared with placebo, and a systematic review found reduced stress and anxiety at 200–400 mg a day. Studies are small and short and some are industry-linked, but L-theanine is a tea compound with a good safety record and no sedation, which makes it the lowest-risk calm pick.
- how: 1) Pick a plain L-theanine product with a third-party seal. 2) Take one serving before your hardest part of the day. 3) Pair it with a Calmmax breathing reset rather than using it instead of one.
- conflicts/synergies: Same compound as in `caffeine_theanine`; if both are active, count total theanine for the day and keep it within the studied range. Pairs with Calmmax `physiological_sigh` and `outdoor_walk`.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6836118/

### `magnesium_glycinate` — Magnesium glycinate
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: dinner (or wind_down)
- frequency: daily
- tier: medium
- programs: sleepmax, calmmax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: kidney disease, antibiotics, osteoporosis meds
- dose note: Supplements commonly provide 100–350 mg of elemental magnesium (check "elemental" on the label, not the compound weight). The upper limit for magnesium from supplements is 350 mg a day for adults; take the serving on your label and no more.
- timing: With dinner or 30–60 min before bed. Keep 2 hours away from tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics and osteoporosis medicines, and 4 hours from thyroid medicine. Loose stools mean the dose is too high for you.
- why: Three small trials in older adults found magnesium helped people fall asleep about 17 min faster, but the evidence was rated low quality; a review of magnesium for stress and anxiety found a possible benefit in anxiety-prone people, also low quality. It is cheap and low risk at label doses for people with healthy kidneys, and many adults eat less than the recommended amount. Glycinate is a commonly chosen form, but no form has strong evidence over another for sleep.
- how: 1) Choose a magnesium glycinate product that lists elemental magnesium. 2) Take it with dinner or in the wind-down. 3) If your stomach is upset, drop to a half serving or stop.
- conflicts/synergies: Check `supp_current_stack`: multivitamins and many "sleep blends" already contain magnesium, so count the total. Keep apart from `iron`. Supports Sleepmax and Calmmax as an optional second pick, never the default.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053283/

### `glycine` — Glycine before bed
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: pre_bed (30–60 min before bed)
- frequency: nightly
- tier: medium
- programs: sleepmax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, antipsychotics
- dose note: Studies used 3 g before bed, usually as a powder stirred into water. Take the serving on your label.
- timing: 30–60 min before bed in a small glass of water.
- why: Small studies from one research group found 3 g of glycine before bed improved how rested people felt, and reduced next-day fatigue after short sleep. The evidence is thin and short-term, but glycine is an amino acid found in food and is well tolerated at this amount. It is an option for people who do not want melatonin.
- how: 1) Buy plain glycine powder. 2) Stir one serving into water before your wind-down. 3) Keep it only if you notice a difference after 2 weeks.
- conflicts/synergies: Can replace `melatonin` for users who decline it or match its avoid-if list. Glycine can blunt the antipsychotic clozapine, hence the antipsychotics flag.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3328957/

### `ashwagandha` — Ashwagandha (caution)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: dinner
- frequency: daily for up to 8–12 weeks, then stop and review (only if the user asks and clears the avoid-if list)
- tier: hard
- programs: calmmax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, thyroid, liver disease, autoimmune condition, sedatives, SSRIs, diabetes meds, blood pressure meds, immunosuppressants, surgery soon, prostate cancer
- dose note: Most trials used about 250–600 mg a day of a standardized root extract for 6–12 weeks. Take no more than the label serving, and do not use it longer than about 3 months without talking to a clinician.
- timing: With a meal, usually dinner since some people feel drowsy. Stop at least 2 weeks before planned surgery.
- why: Several small randomized trials report lower stress and anxiety scores and modestly better sleep with root extract. But NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements notes it may cause liver problems and change thyroid hormone levels, and the NIH LiverTox database lists it as a cause of rare but sometimes severe liver injury. The evidence is real but the risks are too, so it is never a default and is only added when the user asks for it and confirms none of the flags apply.
- how: 1) Use a root-extract product with a third-party seal. 2) Take one serving at the set time; stop after 12 weeks and see how you feel. 3) Stop right away and see a doctor for dark urine, yellow skin or eyes, itching, or belly pain.
- conflicts/synergies: Do not stack with `rhodiola` or other "adaptogen" or "stress" blends. L-theanine (`l_theanine`) is the Calmmax default because it is lower risk.
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Ashwagandha-HealthProfessional/

### `tart_cherry` — Tart cherry (weak evidence)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast and pre_bed (1–2 hours before bed)
- frequency: daily (only if the user asks)
- tier: hard
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: diabetes meds
- dose note: The small sleep studies used about 240 ml (8 oz) of tart cherry juice twice a day. Capsule and concentrate doses vary and are not well studied; follow the label.
- timing: Morning and 1–2 hours before bed. Juice adds sugar and calories, so count it as a drink, not a free extra.
- why: The sleep evidence is weak: two pilot studies of 15 and 8 older adults with insomnia found modest gains, and a study in 20 young adults found higher melatonin levels and modest sleep gains. Reviews on muscle soreness after hard exercise are mixed. It is listed so the AI can answer questions honestly; it is never auto-added and is not tagged to any program.
- how: 1) The evidence is small, so treat it as a 2-week trial. 2) Prefer unsweetened juice or a third-party tested capsule. 3) Keep it only if you notice a difference after 2 weeks.
- conflicts/synergies: Melatonin-related claims are overstated in marketing; it is not a melatonin substitute.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20438325/

### `caffeine_theanine` — L-theanine with your coffee
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast or the coffee before the focus block (never after the caffeine cutoff)
- frequency: workdays (on deep-work days)
- tier: core
- programs: focusmax
- primary for: focusmax
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, heart rhythm problems, blood pressure meds, stimulant meds
- dose note: Studies paired roughly 40–150 mg of caffeine (about one cup of coffee is 80–100 mg) with about 100–250 mg of L-theanine. Your caffeine comes from the coffee or tea you already drink; the add-on is one L-theanine serving from your label. The FDA cites 400 mg of caffeine a day as an amount not generally linked to problems in healthy adults; this habit never adds caffeine.
- timing: With the coffee or tea before your deep-work block, 20–30 min ahead. Never later than 8 hours before bed (6 at the very least), following the shared caffeine cutoff.
- why: In placebo-controlled trials the combination improved accuracy when switching between tasks and made people feel more alert for an hour or two, and a meta-analysis of tea compounds found the pairing helped attention more than either alone. Effects are modest and short-lived. This is the lowest-risk way to use caffeine for focus: no caffeine pills, energy drinks or extra cups.
- how: 1) Keep your normal coffee or tea, no extra caffeine. 2) Take one L-theanine serving with it before the block. 3) Skip it on days without focused work or if you feel jittery.
- conflicts/synergies: Attach to Focusmax `caffeine_for_focus` and bound by the Sleepmax/Energymax `caffeine_cutoff`. Do not pair with pre-workout drinks or energy drinks. If `l_theanine` is also active, merge into one theanine dose where the times are close and keep the daily total in the studied range.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24946991/

### `creatine` — Creatine monohydrate
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: workout (or breakfast on rest days)
- frequency: daily, including rest days
- tier: core
- programs: trainmax
- primary for: trainmax
- avoid if: kidney disease, under 18, pregnancy, breastfeeding
- dose note: Most labels: 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate a day; take the serving on your label. A "loading" phase is not needed; daily use reaches the same level in about 3–4 weeks.
- timing: Any time of day; consistency matters more than timing. Easiest after a workout or with breakfast in water, a shake or coffee.
- why: The International Society of Sports Nutrition calls creatine monohydrate the most effective supplement for high-intensity exercise capacity and lean mass when combined with training, and finds it safe long term in healthy people. Some meta-analyses also find small memory benefits. It can add 1–2 kg of water weight at first, and it raises blood creatinine, a kidney lab value, so tell your doctor before bloodwork.
- how: 1) Buy plain creatine monohydrate with an NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport seal. 2) Stir one serving into any drink once a day. 3) Keep taking it on rest days.
- conflicts/synergies: Pairs with Fitmax `strength_full_body` and `post_workout_meal`. No benefit from fancier forms. Avoid "proprietary blend" pre-workouts that hide caffeine.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5469049/

### `protein_powder` — Protein shake (food first)
- minutes: 2 / 2
- anchor: workout (within a few hours after) or a low-protein meal
- frequency: training days or when meals fall short
- tier: medium
- programs: trainmax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: kidney disease, milk allergy
- dose note: A typical scoop has about 20–30 g of protein; ISSN suggests 20–40 g per serving is enough to support muscle repair. Use one serving on your label to fill a gap, not on top of protein-rich meals.
- timing: After training or alongside a meal that is light on protein. There is no strict 30-minute window.
- why: A meta-analysis found extra protein during strength training improved muscle and strength gains, with little added benefit past a total daily intake of about 1.6 g per kg body weight. Total daily protein from food matters most; powder is a convenience for days when meals fall short. Third-party testing matters because protein powders can contain contaminants.
- how: 1) Try to get protein at each meal first (Fitmax habit). 2) Use one scoop after training if a meal is far off. 3) Pick a product with NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport.
- conflicts/synergies: Same lever as Fitmax `protein_each_meal` and `post_workout_meal` and Energymax `protein_first_meal`; never schedule as a separate task when a protein meal already sits there. Do not frame as a gram-per-kg prescription. Plant powders work for users with a milk allergy.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28698222/

### `electrolytes` — Electrolytes on sweaty days
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: workout (during or after) or the morning water on hot days
- frequency: on workout days over ~60 min, hot days or heavy-sweat days (usually 2–4 times a week); not every day by default
- tier: core
- programs: energymax, trainmax
- primary for: energymax
- avoid if: kidney disease, blood pressure meds, heart failure, heart meds
- dose note: Products vary widely, often 200–1,000 mg of sodium per serving, plus potassium. One serving per sweaty session or hot day is usually enough; take the serving on your label. Pick low- or no-sugar versions.
- timing: During or after a long or sweaty workout, or with the first big glass of water on a hot day. Not close to bedtime (more night-time bathroom trips).
- why: In lab studies, mild dehydration (about 1–1.5% of body weight) lowered mood, raised fatigue and made concentration harder. Electrolytes replace the sodium lost in sweat and make it easier to drink enough. On ordinary desk days, plain water is enough for most people. It is the Energymax default because it is the lowest-risk supplement with a plausible energy link and it is not a stimulant; Energymax rules out caffeine pills and energy drinks.
- how: 1) Keep a few single-serve, low-sugar sachets at hand. 2) Use one in your water on hot or sweaty days. 3) On normal days, plain water is enough.
- conflicts/synergies: Pairs with Energymax `water_on_waking` and `water_bottle_refill`, and Fitmax sessions. People on low-sodium diets or blood pressure medicine should ask their clinician first.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/

### `rhodiola` — Rhodiola (mixed evidence)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast
- frequency: daily for a few weeks (only if the user asks)
- tier: hard
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: pregnancy, breastfeeding, under 18, bipolar disorder, SSRIs, stimulant meds, diabetes meds, blood pressure meds, immunosuppressants
- dose note: Trials used a wide range, mostly about 200–600 mg a day of a standardized root extract for a few weeks. Take no more than the label serving.
- timing: Morning only, ideally before or with breakfast. It can be stimulating, so never within 8 hours of bed.
- why: A systematic review found the evidence for rhodiola against physical or mental fatigue inconclusive, and NIH's NCCIH says there is not enough reliable evidence to judge it; product quality and adulteration are also a known problem. It is listed so the AI can answer honestly. It is never auto-added and is not tagged to Energymax, whose doc rules out stimulant-type products.
- how: 1) The evidence is mixed, so treat it as a short trial. 2) Use a third-party tested product. 3) Stop if it causes jitteriness, trouble sleeping or mood changes.
- conflicts/synergies: Do not combine with `ashwagandha`, caffeine pills or other stimulants. `caffeine_theanine` timing rules apply.
- source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3541197/

### `collagen_peptides` — Collagen peptides (12-week trial)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast (stirred into coffee, a smoothie or oats)
- frequency: daily for a 12-week trial, then keep only if the user sees a difference
- tier: medium
- programs: skinhairmax
- primary for: skinhairmax
- avoid if: fish allergy, pregnancy, breastfeeding
- dose note: Most trials used about 2.5–10 g of hydrolyzed collagen peptides a day for 8–12 weeks. Take the serving on your label.
- timing: Once a day, any time; easiest with breakfast. Dissolves in hot or cold drinks.
- why: Honest picture: a 2023 meta-analysis of 26 trials found better skin hydration and elasticity, but a 2025 meta-analysis found those benefits disappeared when it looked only at trials not funded by industry or at higher-quality trials. There is no good evidence it helps hair. It is the Skin & Hair default only because it is very low risk (it is a food protein) and it has been studied specifically for skin. Sunscreen and a retinoid (Skin & Hair habits) do far more. If the user would rather skip it, that is a reasonable choice.
- how: 1) Pick an unflavored hydrolyzed collagen with a third-party seal (marine collagen if you avoid beef or pork; skip it with a fish allergy). 2) Stir one serving into breakfast. 3) Take a photo at week 0 and week 12 and decide.
- conflicts/synergies: Never replaces Skin & Hair `am_spf` or `pm_retinoid`. Counts toward daily protein. Do not add biotin "hair" supplements: high-dose biotin can distort lab tests and there is no good evidence it helps people who are not deficient.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37432180/

### `vitamin_d3` — Vitamin D3 with a meal
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast or lunch (the meal with some fat)
- frequency: daily
- tier: core
- programs: longevitymax
- primary for: longevitymax
- avoid if: kidney disease, high calcium, kidney stones, sarcoidosis, water pills, heart meds
- dose note: The adult recommended intake is 600 IU (15 mcg) a day, 800 IU over age 70, and the upper limit from all sources is 4,000 IU (100 mcg). Common labels are 1,000–2,000 IU. Take the serving on your label; a doctor can check your blood level if you are unsure.
- timing: With a meal that contains some fat, at the same meal each day, so it joins the breakfast stack.
- why: Many adults get less vitamin D from food than recommended, and it works with calcium for bone and muscle. Be honest about limits: in the VITAL trial of about 26,000 adults, 2,000 IU a day did not lower cancer or heart disease overall, though it was linked to fewer new autoimmune diagnoses. It is the Longevitymax default because it covers a common gap, costs little and is low risk at label doses, not because it extends life. Most useful for people with little sun, darker skin, older age or a known low level.
- how: 1) Pick D3 at 1,000–2,000 IU with a USP or NSF seal. 2) Take it with breakfast or lunch. 3) Check your multivitamin so the total stays under the upper limit.
- conflicts/synergies: Check `multivitamin` and `supp_current_stack` for vitamin D already in other products. Never suggest skipping sunscreen to "get vitamin D" (Skin & Hair rule). Pairs with Longevitymax `checkup_reminder` (ask about a level).
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/

### `omega3` — Omega-3 (fish or algae oil)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast or dinner (with a meal)
- frequency: daily
- tier: medium
- programs: longevitymax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: blood thinners, surgery soon, fish allergy, heart rhythm problems
- dose note: Typical labels provide about 250–1,000 mg of EPA plus DHA per serving. Trials found a higher risk of atrial fibrillation (an irregular heartbeat) at doses above 1 g a day, so stay at or under 1 g of EPA plus DHA a day unless a clinician says otherwise. Algae oil is the vegetarian option.
- timing: With a meal that contains fat; taking it with food also reduces fishy burps.
- why: Two servings of oily fish a week is the food-first route. In the VITAL trial, 1 g a day did not lower major heart events or cancer overall in healthy adults, while a meta-analysis of large trials found a dose-related rise in atrial fibrillation. It is a reasonable optional pick for people who rarely eat fish, not a default.
- how: 1) Try oily fish twice a week first. 2) If you rarely eat fish, pick a third-party tested fish or algae oil. 3) Take it with dinner or breakfast.
- conflicts/synergies: Mild blood-thinning effect, so it is flagged for blood thinners and surgery. Pairs with `vitamin_d3` in the same meal slot.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34612056/

### `psyllium_fiber` — Psyllium fiber
- minutes: 1 / 2
- anchor: lunch or dinner (with a full glass of water)
- frequency: daily (start every other day in week 1)
- tier: medium
- programs: longevitymax
- primary for: none
- avoid if: trouble swallowing, bowel obstruction
- dose note: Label servings are usually about 3–5 g of psyllium husk, 1–3 times a day; cholesterol trials used about 10 g a day. Start with one serving and build up over 1–2 weeks so your gut can adjust.
- timing: With a meal and at least 240 ml (8 oz) of water. Keep it 2 hours away from medicines and other supplements, because it can slow their absorption.
- why: Most adults eat far less fiber than recommended. A meta-analysis of 28 trials found about 10 g of psyllium a day lowered LDL cholesterol by roughly 13 mg/dL, and it also helps regularity. Food first: the Longevitymax `fiber_swap` habit comes before a powder.
- how: 1) Stir one serving into a full glass of water and drink it right away. 2) Take it with lunch or dinner, apart from pills. 3) Add a second serving only after a week without bloating.
- conflicts/synergies: Synergy with Longevitymax `fiber_swap` and `veg_first`. Never schedule in the same slot as other supplements or medicines.
- source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239559/

### `multivitamin` — Basic multivitamin
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast
- frequency: daily (only when the user's diet or goal calls for it)
- tier: medium
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: blood thinners, pregnancy, smoking
- dose note: A basic multivitamin at about 100% of the Daily Value for most nutrients; skip "mega" or high-potency formulas. Take the serving on your label.
- timing: With breakfast or the largest meal (the fat helps absorb vitamins A, D, E and K). Keep it 2 hours apart from `iron` and thyroid medicine if it contains calcium.
- why: NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements notes that most people who eat a varied diet get what they need from food, and large trials in healthy adults (including COSMOS) found no effect on cancer or heart disease. A basic multi can fill gaps for people with restrictive diets, low appetite or older age. Pregnancy needs a prenatal chosen with a clinician instead; people who smoke should avoid high-beta-carotene formulas; vitamin K can interfere with warfarin.
- how: 1) Pick a basic formula with a USP seal. 2) Take it with breakfast. 3) Check it against your other products for doubled vitamin D, iron or biotin.
- conflicts/synergies: Overlaps with `vitamin_d3`, `b12`, `magnesium_glycinate` and `iron`; add up totals and stay under upper limits. Not a substitute for food.
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/MVMS-HealthProfessional/

### `b12` — Vitamin B12 (vegans, 50+)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: breakfast
- frequency: daily (only when it applies: vegan or vegetarian, over 50, or long-term metformin or acid reducers)
- tier: medium
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: none
- dose note: The adult recommended intake is 2.4 mcg a day. Supplements often contain 25–1,000 mcg because only part of a pill dose is absorbed; no upper limit has been set. Take the serving on your label.
- timing: With breakfast; any time works.
- why: B12 occurs naturally only in animal foods, so vegans and many vegetarians need fortified foods or a supplement. People over 50 absorb less B12 from food, and long-term metformin or acid-reducing medicines can lower levels. Extra B12 does not raise energy in people who are not low.
- how: 1) Pick a third-party tested B12; any form works. 2) Take it with breakfast. 3) If you feel tired or get numbness or tingling, ask your doctor for a blood test.
- conflicts/synergies: Often already in a `multivitamin`; check before adding. Never pitched as an energy booster.
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/

### `iron` — Iron (bloodwork only)
- minutes: 1 / 1
- anchor: wake or mid-morning (on an empty stomach, away from coffee and tea)
- frequency: as a clinician directs after a blood test (often every other day); only when the user says a blood test showed low iron and a clinician advised iron
- tier: hard
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: no bloodwork, hemochromatosis, under 18, thyroid, pregnancy
- dose note: Only the amount a clinician recommends after bloodwork confirms low iron. The upper limit for adults not under medical care is 45 mg of elemental iron a day. Suppmax never sets an iron dose.
- timing: On an empty stomach or with a little vitamin-C-rich food, and at least 2 hours away from coffee, tea, dairy, calcium, zinc, magnesium, antacids and thyroid medicine. Every-other-day dosing improved absorption in a randomized trial; follow the clinician.
- why: Low iron is a real and common cause of fatigue, especially for people who menstruate, but iron you do not need can upset the stomach and build up in the body, and iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6. That is why it is never auto-added and is not tagged to any program, including Energymax.
- how: 1) Take the dose your clinician set. 2) Take it on its own, away from the breakfast stack (coffee, tea and calcium block it). 3) Store it out of children's reach.
- conflicts/synergies: Must not share a slot with `multivitamin` (calcium), `magnesium_glycinate`, coffee or tea. If the user is tired and asks about iron, suggest bloodwork, not a supplement.
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/

### `stack_audit` — One-time stack audit
- minutes: 5 / 10
- anchor: weekend (first weekend)
- frequency: once in week 1, then whenever a medicine changes
- tier: core
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: none
- dose note: n/a (a review, not a supplement)
- timing: First weekend, before adding anything new beyond the week-1 picks.
- why: People often double up without knowing it (vitamin D in a multi plus a D3, magnesium in a sleep blend, caffeine in a pre-workout). NIH advises telling your doctors and pharmacist about every supplement you take, because some interact with medicines.
- how: 1) Line up every pill, powder, gummy and drink mix you take. 2) Photograph the labels and note the doses; the chat can flag duplicates. 3) Show the list to a pharmacist or doctor, especially if you take prescription medicine.
- conflicts/synergies: Required first step if the user reports any regular medication or more than 3 current products.
- source: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/WYNTK-Consumer/

### `weekly_stack_checkin` — Weekly stack check-in
- minutes: 2 / 3
- anchor: weekend
- frequency: weekly
- tier: core
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: none
- dose note: n/a
- timing: Same weekend slot as other weekly reviews.
- why: A quick weekly check catches side effects early, shows whether anything is actually helping, and keeps time-limited items (melatonin, ashwagandha, collagen trial) from running on forever. The FDA asks people to report serious reactions to supplements.
- how: 1) Rate how consistently you took each item. 2) Note any side effects (stomach upset, sleep change, jitteriness, rash). 3) Pause anything with side effects and tell the chat; for anything serious, stop and get medical care.
- conflicts/synergies: Merge with Sleepmax `sleep_log` review or Focusmax `weekly_review` if both are on.
- source: https://www.fda.gov/food/dietary-supplements

### `refill_check` — Refill and label check
- minutes: 2 / 2
- anchor: weekend
- frequency: every 2–4 weeks (when a bottle is about a week from empty)
- tier: medium
- programs: none
- primary for: none
- avoid if: none
- dose note: n/a
- timing: Weekend, before the bottle runs out.
- why: Running out is the most common reason routines lapse, and switching brands mid-plan can change the dose. Seals such as USP Verified, NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Choice/Informed Sport mean an independent lab checked what is in the product.
- how: 1) Check what is running low and the expiry dates. 2) Reorder the same third-party tested product. 3) Store bottles cool, dry and out of children's reach.
- conflicts/synergies: None.
- source: https://www.nsfsport.com/

## What the AI needs to know (intake)
The general profile already has wake/bed, meals, workout time, goals and active programs. Ask only these, in order. The safety questions come first and cannot be skipped; a skipped or "prefer not to say" answer is treated as cautious (schedule only `stack_audit` and suggest a pharmacist check).

1. `supp_safety_flags` (required, multi) — Removes anything whose `avoid if` matches.
   Q: "Before we pick anything: do any of these apply to you?" Options: None of these | Pregnant, trying, or breastfeeding | Kidney or liver condition | Surgery in the next 2 weeks | Under 18
   Maps to avoid-if keywords: pregnant/breastfeeding → pregnancy, breastfeeding; kidney or liver → kidney disease, liver disease, kidney stones; surgery → surgery soon; under 18 → under 18.
2. `supp_medications` (required, multi) — Removes interacting items and triggers `stack_audit` plus a pharmacist suggestion.
   Q: "Do you take any of these regularly?" Options: None | Blood thinners | Antidepressants, anxiety or sleep meds | Blood pressure or heart meds | Diabetes, thyroid or other daily meds
   Maps to: blood thinners → blood thinners; antidepressants/anxiety/sleep → SSRIs, sedatives, antipsychotics, bipolar disorder; blood pressure or heart → blood pressure meds, heart meds, water pills, heart rhythm problems, heart failure; diabetes/thyroid/other → diabetes meds, thyroid, immunosuppressants, seizure meds, stimulant meds, antibiotics, osteoporosis meds (treat all as possible; ask one follow-up in chat if a tagged default would be removed).
3. `supp_current_stack` (required, multi) — Prevents doubling up and lets the plan schedule what they already own.
   Q: "What do you already take?" Options: Nothing yet | Multivitamin | Vitamin D or fish oil | Magnesium, melatonin or a sleep aid | Protein, creatine or pre-workout
4. `supp_daily_max` (optional, default 3) — Caps how many supplements the plan schedules per day.
   Q: "How many supplements a day will you realistically take?" Options: 1–2 | 3–4 | 5 or more | As few as possible

If only three questions can be asked, ask 1, 2 and 3 and use a daily max of 3. Keywords not covered by intake (fish allergy, milk allergy, autoimmune condition, prostate cancer, hemochromatosis, high calcium, sarcoidosis, smoking, trouble swallowing, bowel obstruction) are matched against `known_facts` and anything the user says in chat. Before adding any `tier: hard` item, confirm its avoid-if list with one yes/no question. `iron` additionally requires the user to say a blood test showed low iron (`no bloodwork` matches until they do).

## Scheduling rules
- Sync with other programs (the core rule):
  - For each active program other than Suppmax, find the habit whose `primary for` is that program and add it, unless (a) any `avoid if` keyword matches the user's answers or known facts, (b) the user declined it before, or (c) the user already takes it (then schedule their own product as a reminder instead of adding a second one).
  - When that program is removed, remove its synced supplement, unless the user chose it themselves for their own goal (added by name, or said "keep it"). Store the reason an item is in the plan (`synced:<program>` or `user`) so removal is exact.
  - Habits listing a program under `programs` but not as `primary for` are optional extras for that program: suggest them only when the user asks for more, or when the default is ruled out (e.g. `glycine` or `magnesium_glycinate` when `melatonin` matches an avoid-if). Extras added this way are linked to that program and removed with it unless the user asks to keep them.
  - `programs: none` items are never added by sync. Add them only when the user asks or their own facts call for it (vegan or 50+ → `b12`; limited diet → `multivitamin`; clinician-confirmed low iron → `iron`).
  - If a default is removed by a safety match, say so plainly ("left out melatonin because of your sleep medicine; ask your pharmacist"), never silently.
  - With no other programs active, offer the defaults that match the user's onboarding goals, one yes per item; nothing is auto-added.
- Daily cap: week 1 schedules at most 3 supplements (or `supp_daily_max` if lower), plus `stack_audit` on the first weekend. Add 1 per week if adherence is 70% or more, up to `supp_daily_max` and never more than 5 a day. If synced defaults exceed the cap, add them in order of the user's top goal first, then program start order, and queue the rest with a note.
- Group into as few reminder slots as possible (aim for 1–3 a day):
  - With breakfast: `vitamin_d3`, `omega3`, `multivitamin`, `b12`, `collagen_peptides`, `caffeine_theanine` (if the coffee is at breakfast), `creatine` on rest days.
  - Around the workout: `creatine`, `protein_powder`, `electrolytes`.
  - Afternoon: `l_theanine`, or `caffeine_theanine` before an afternoon focus block if still before the cutoff.
  - With dinner: `magnesium_glycinate`, `ashwagandha`, `psyllium_fiber` (only if no medicines are taken at dinner; otherwise lunch).
  - Before bed: `melatonin` (30–60 min before), `glycine`.
- Fat-soluble items (`vitamin_d3`, `omega3`, `multivitamin`) go with a meal that contains some fat.
- Spacing:
  - `iron` gets its own slot, at least 2 hours from coffee, tea, dairy, calcium, zinc, magnesium, antacids, `multivitamin` and thyroid medicine.
  - Calcium-, magnesium- and zinc-containing products are kept 2 hours apart from each other where possible, and 4 hours from thyroid medicine.
  - `magnesium_glycinate` 2 hours away from tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics and osteoporosis medicines.
  - `psyllium_fiber` 2 hours away from all medicines and other supplements.
- Stimulating items (`caffeine_theanine`, `rhodiola`) never within 8 hours of bedtime (6 at the very least), sharing the Sleepmax/Energymax `caffeine_cutoff`. Never schedule caffeine pills, energy drinks or more than one stimulating item a day.
- Sedating items (`melatonin`, `glycine`, `ashwagandha`) only in the evening; never before driving.
- Time limits: `melatonin` 4 weeks then a week off and review; `ashwagandha` 8–12 weeks then stop and review; `collagen_peptides` 12-week trial; `tart_cherry` and `glycine` reviewed at 2 weeks.
- Supplements count as 1 minute each and do not eat into other programs' time budgets, but they count toward the visible daily task cap.
- Weekly: `weekly_stack_checkin` every weekend; `refill_check` every 2–4 weeks.

## Safety and claims
- Supplements are not approved by the FDA for safety or effectiveness before they are sold, and label contents can be wrong. Prefer products with an independent seal: USP Verified, NSF Certified for Sport, or Informed Choice/Informed Sport.
- Never claim a supplement treats, cures, mitigates or prevents a disease (FTC and DSHEA rules). Use "studied for", "may help", "the evidence is mixed". Say when evidence is weak.
- Dose notes are information about what studies and labels use, not prescriptions. Never schedule more than the label serving, never suggest megadoses, and keep totals across products under the NIH upper limits (vitamin D 4,000 IU, supplemental magnesium 350 mg, iron 45 mg).
- Tell the user to talk to their doctor or pharmacist before starting, especially with any prescription medicine, pregnancy or breastfeeding, a chronic condition, or upcoming surgery. Do not suggest stopping or changing any medication.
- Never add `iron` without bloodwork and a clinician's advice. Never add anything for under-18s that is flagged `under 18` (creatine, ashwagandha, rhodiola, melatonin, caffeine pairings, iron and others).
- No stimulant stacking: no caffeine pills, energy drinks, pre-workouts with hidden caffeine, or combining `rhodiola` with `caffeine_theanine`.
- No prescription drugs, peptides, hormones, SARMs, nootropic stacks, NAD+/NMN, high-dose biotin or "proprietary blends" that hide doses.
- If the user reports a reaction (rash, swelling, trouble breathing, chest pain, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, severe stomach pain, a racing heart), tell them to stop the product and seek medical care; for trouble breathing or chest pain, call emergency services. Serious reactions can be reported to the FDA.
- A supplement never replaces a clinician for ongoing insomnia, anxiety, low mood, fatigue or hair loss.
