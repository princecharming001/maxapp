# LongevityMax

## What it is
LongevityMax is a short list of everyday basics that add up over years: easy cardio, strength twice a week, daily steps, vegetables and fiber first, a regular sleep schedule, time with people, and reminders to keep up with routine checkups. None of it is exotic; the aim is to make the proven basics automatic in a busy week. It gives no medical advice and makes no lifespan promises.

## Who it fits / who should check with a professional first
Fits: adults of any age who want a simple, sustainable "health basics" routine rather than gadgets or supplements.

Check with a professional first:
- Heart disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, diabetes, kidney disease, recent surgery, or symptoms like chest pain, fainting or unusual breathlessness with effort: talk with a doctor about the right type and amount of activity before starting cardio or strength work.
- Digestive conditions (IBS, IBD) or a history of bowel obstruction: increase fiber only with clinician guidance; raise slowly.
- Pregnancy: activity and fiber are generally fine but follow the clinician's plan.
- History of eating disorders: skip food-rule framing; keep only gentle "add vegetables" habits or none.
- Over 65 or with balance concerns: include balance work; avoid jumping.

## Habit library

### `zone2_cardio` — Easy cardio (zone 2)
- minutes: 20 / 30 (up to 45)
- anchor: before_work, lunch, after_work or commute
- frequency: 2–4× per week (toward 150 min/week of moderate activity in total, counting walks)
- tier: core
- why: Higher cardiorespiratory fitness was linked to much lower long-term mortality in a 122,000-person cohort, with no upper limit to the benefit.
- how: 1) Brisk walk, bike, swim, row or easy jog. 2) Keep a "can talk, can't sing" pace. 3) Build from 20 to 30–45 min over a few weeks.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as FitMax `zone2_cardio`; schedule once. Moderate evening cardio is generally fine for sleep.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.3605

### `strength_2x` — Strength twice a week
- minutes: 20 / 30
- anchor: user's workout time, or before_work / after_work
- frequency: 2× per week, ≥48 h apart
- tier: core
- why: Adults are advised to do muscle-strengthening activity for all major muscle groups at least 2 days a week.
- how: 1) 4–6 full-body moves (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry, core). 2) 2–3 sets of 8–12 reps. 3) Add reps or load slowly.
- conflicts/synergies: If FitMax is active, FitMax sessions satisfy this; do not double-schedule.
- source: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html

### `daily_steps` — Daily steps
- minutes: 0 / 0 (accumulated)
- anchor: all day; commute, calls, post-meal walks
- frequency: daily
- tier: core
- why: In a pooled analysis of 15 cohorts, more daily steps were linked to lower mortality, leveling off around 6,000–8,000 steps for adults 60+ and 8,000–10,000 for younger adults.
- how: 1) Learn your current average. 2) Add about 1,000 per day every 1–2 weeks. 3) Use walking calls, stairs and post-meal walks.
- conflicts/synergies: Shared with FitMax and EnergyMax; count once.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00302-9

### `stair_snack` — Vigorous minute
- minutes: 1 / 2
- anchor: work_break or commute
- frequency: 1–3× per day
- tier: medium
- why: Brief bursts of vigorous daily activity (about 3 bouts of 1–2 min a day) were linked to lower mortality in people who did not exercise.
- how: 1) Take stairs fast or walk uphill briskly for about 1 min. 2) Or carry groceries quickly. 3) Easy recovery.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as EnergyMax `vigorous_burst` / FitMax `stair_or_burst`. Not in the last hour before bed. Skip if heart flags unless cleared.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-02100-x

### `veg_first` — Vegetables first
- minutes: 0 / 2
- anchor: lunch and dinner
- frequency: daily (at least one meal)
- tier: core
- why: Higher fiber intake (about 25–29 g/day or more) was linked to lower risk of death and several chronic diseases; most adults eat about half that.
- how: 1) Start lunch or dinner with vegetables or a salad. 2) Aim for half the plate as vegetables. 3) Keep frozen vegetables on hand for busy nights.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as EnergyMax `lunch_balance`. Raise fiber gradually with water to avoid discomfort.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31809-9

### `fiber_swap` — One fiber swap
- minutes: 0 / 2
- anchor: get_ready, lunch or dinner
- frequency: daily
- tier: medium
- why: Swapping refined grains for whole grains, beans and lentils is the simplest way to raise fiber.
- how: 1) Pick one swap: white → whole-grain bread or rice, add beans or lentils to a meal, oats at breakfast, fruit or nuts as a snack. 2) Add one new swap per week. 3) Drink water with it.
- conflicts/synergies: Pairs with EnergyMax `protein_first_meal` (oats + yogurt).
- source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(18)31809-9

### `sleep_regularity` — Regular sleep window
- minutes: 0 / 0 (a rule)
- anchor: wake and pre_bed
- frequency: daily
- tier: core
- why: In 60,000+ UK Biobank adults, more regular sleep timing was linked to lower all-cause and cardiometabolic mortality, more strongly than sleep length.
- how: 1) Keep wake time within about 1 hour every day. 2) Keep bedtime similar. 3) Use SleepMax habits if this is hard.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as SleepMax `fixed_wake_time` + `weekend_anchor`; count once.
- source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10782489/

### `social_connection` — Connect with someone
- minutes: 5 / 15
- anchor: commute, lunch, after_work or weekend
- frequency: 3–5× per week (at least 1 in-person plan per week)
- tier: core
- why: A meta-analysis of 148 studies found stronger social relationships linked to about 50% higher odds of survival, comparable to well-known risk factors.
- how: 1) Call or voice-note one person. 2) Book a recurring walk, meal or class with someone. 3) Say yes to one invite this week.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as CalmMax `connect_ping`. Combine with walks (walk-and-talk).
- source: https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316

### `checkup_reminder` — Routine checkup reminder
- minutes: 5 / 10 (to book, not to attend)
- anchor: weekend
- frequency: 1× per year (plus a nudge if the user says they're overdue)
- tier: core
- why: Preventive visits, screenings and vaccines can find problems before symptoms; which ones you need depends on age, sex and history.
- how: 1) Use the MyHealthfinder tool or ask your clinician what is due for you. 2) Book a yearly checkup and any due screenings. 3) Add dental and eye checks.
- conflicts/synergies: Pairs with SkinHair `skin_self_check`. The app never decides which screenings the user needs.
- source: https://odphp.health.gov/myhealthfinder

### `balance_practice` — Balance practice
- minutes: 2 / 3
- anchor: get_ready (while brushing teeth)
- frequency: 3–5× per week
- tier: medium (core for 60+)
- why: Guidelines recommend balance training for older adults, and it stacks easily on daily routines.
- how: 1) Stand on one leg 30 s per side near a counter. 2) Progress to heel-to-toe walking. 3) Hold on if unsteady.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as FitMax `balance_drill`.
- source: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html

### `nature_time` — Weekly nature time
- minutes: 20 / 30
- anchor: weekend or lunch
- frequency: aim for about 120 min/week total (can be split)
- tier: medium
- why: People with at least 120 min of nature contact a week more often reported good health and wellbeing.
- how: 1) Walk in a park, by water or on a trail. 2) Split into 3–4 shorter visits if needed. 3) Bring someone.
- conflicts/synergies: Counts as CalmMax `outdoor_walk` and step time.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-44097-3

### `alcohol_awareness` — Alcohol-free days
- minutes: 0 / 0
- anchor: dinner
- frequency: shown only if the user drinks; aim for more alcohol-free days per week
- tier: medium
- why: Health agencies advise that drinking less is better for health than drinking more, and alcohol disrupts sleep quality.
- how: 1) Choose set alcohol-free days. 2) Have a go-to non-alcoholic drink. 3) If you drink, finish 3+ hours before bed.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as SleepMax `alcohol_buffer`. Never encourage drinking.
- source: https://www.cdc.gov/alcohol/about-alcohol-use/moderate-alcohol-use.html

### `move_after_sitting` — Break up long sitting
- minutes: 2 / 3
- anchor: work_break (every 60–90 min)
- frequency: 3–6× per workday
- tier: core
- why: Short walking breaks during long sitting lowered post-meal blood sugar compared with uninterrupted sitting.
- how: 1) Set a 60–90 min timer. 2) Walk 2–3 min. 3) Return to work.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as EnergyMax `movement_snack` / FitMax `posture_break`; merge.
- source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4

### `weekly_long_walk` — Weekend long walk
- minutes: 30 / 60
- anchor: weekend
- frequency: 1× per week
- tier: hard
- why: Adds easy aerobic volume, nature time and often social time in one block.
- how: 1) Pick a hike, long walk or bike ride. 2) Conversational pace. 3) Invite someone.
- conflicts/synergies: Same as FitMax `long_walk_weekend`, CalmMax `weekend_unplug`.
- source: https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html

## What the AI needs to know (intake)
1. `age_band` (required) — Changes step targets (6,000–8,000 for 60+ vs 8,000–10,000 under 60), balance priority and impact level. Skip if the general profile already has age.
   Q: "Which age range are you in?" Options: Under 30 | 30–44 | 45–59 | 60+
2. `activity_baseline` (required) — Sets cardio and strength starting doses.
   Q: "How active are you in a normal week?" Options: Mostly sitting | Some walking | Exercise 1–2× a week | Exercise 3+ times a week
3. `health_flags` (required) — Triggers "check with your doctor" and removes vigorous bursts if needed.
   Q: "Has a doctor told you to be careful with exercise, or do you have a heart, lung, or blood-pressure condition?" Options: No | Yes | Not sure
4. `last_checkup` (optional) — Decides whether to show the checkup reminder in week 1.
   Q: "When was your last routine checkup?" Options: Within the last year | 1–2 years ago | Longer or never | Not sure
5. `social_frequency` (optional) — Sets how often connection habits appear.
   Q: "How often do you spend real time with friends or family?" Options: Most days | Weekly | Less than weekly

## Scheduling rules
- Strength: 2× per week, at least 48 h apart (for example Tue/Fri); if FitMax is active, defer to FitMax sessions.
- Cardio: 2–4 sessions of 20–45 min; total moderate activity (cardio + brisk walking) toward 150 min/week. Vigorous efforts end at least 2 hours before bed.
- Steps: start at current average, +1,000/day every 1–2 weeks; target by `age_band`.
- Stair snacks: not within 1 hour of bed; not on intervals days.
- Food habits: anchor to stored lunch and dinner times; fiber increases gradually (one swap per week).
- Sleep regularity: inherit SleepMax rules (wake within 60 min daily, including weekends).
- Social: at least one in-person plan per week (weekend or after_work).
- Checkup reminder: once on a weekend in week 1–2 if `last_checkup` is over a year or unknown; then yearly.
- Week 1 ramp: daily steps baseline, one 20-min cardio session, one strength session, `veg_first` at one meal. Week 2: second strength session, `sleep_regularity`. Week 3+: social, fiber swaps, stair snacks, nature time.
- Scaling: ~5 min/day → steps (passive), veg first, sleep regularity, movement breaks. 15–25 min/day → add 2× strength + 2× cardio. 30+ min/day → add a weekend long walk, nature time and stair snacks.
- Weekend: long walk/nature time, social plan, weekly checkup/booking tasks.
- Deduplicate with FitMax, EnergyMax, SleepMax and CalmMax: each shared habit appears once and credits all programs.

## Safety and claims
- No lifespan promises ("add 10 years"), "reverse aging", biological-age scores, or claims to prevent or cure diseases. Use "linked to" and "associated with", since most evidence is observational.
- No medical advice: do not interpret lab results, blood pressure, cholesterol or wearable data; do not say which screenings or tests a user needs; point to their clinician and MyHealthfinder.
- No supplements (NAD+, metformin, rapamycin, resveratrol, etc.), peptides, hormones, prescription drugs, or off-label use.
- No extended fasting, very-low-calorie diets, or calorie targets. No cold plunges, sauna protocols, or hypoxic training prescriptions.
- Do not encourage alcohol for "heart health".
- Exercise warning signs (chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness): stop and seek medical care.
